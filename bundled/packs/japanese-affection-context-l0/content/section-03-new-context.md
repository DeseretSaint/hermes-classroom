# Lesson 3 — a new context, then explain your choice

Lesson 2 gave you the map. This lesson asks you to use it without the table in front of you.

## Practice 1 — a first confession

You are at a quiet café. You have known the other person for a few weeks, you respect them, and
you are not close yet. You want to say that you like them and ask to see them again.

Write the line you would use. Then write one sentence in English saying which cue in the
situation made you choose it.

## Practice 2 — a settled partnership

You are at home with your spouse of ten years. It is a private moment, and the feeling is deep
rather than new.

Write the line you would use. Then write one sentence saying why this line differs from
Practice 1 — or why it does not.

## Practice 3 — explain the register

In English, explain why a first confession to someone you respect calls for a different line from
a settled partnership. Name the addressee, the relationship and the setting in your answer.

## Practice 4 — a line that does not fit

A learner says: "I will use the strongest line I know, because stronger means more sincere."
In English, explain what this reasoning misses about addressee, relationship and setting.

## After you answer

Your answers are checked as attempts, not as a claim of fluency. A correct answer to one item is
not durable mastery, and a repeated near-identical item is not evidence of transfer. If you miss
one, the next step is a new item that targets the same idea in a different situation.

Romaji may be used in an answer as a scaffold. A romaji-only answer is not evidence that you can
read Japanese script, so read the kana forms in Lesson 2 alongside it.
