# Lesson 2 — choosing by addressee, relationship and setting

There is no single Japanese line that means "I love you". The same feeling is carried by
different lines depending on who is listening, how close you are, and where you are standing.
The English word stays fixed; the Japanese line moves.

## The four lines in this lesson

| Relationship | Setting | Line | Romaji | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| friend | casual, after an outing | 好きだよ | suki da yo | I like you |
| someone you respect but are not close to | private, first confession | 好きです | suki desu | I like you (polite) |
| close partner | everyday, warm | 大好き | daisuki | I really like you |
| long-term partner | private, settled | 愛してる | aishiteru | I love you (intimate) |

Each line is a real option. None of them is a mistranslation; they differ in closeness,
directness and weight.

## What the sources support

- `好き` (suki) is dictionary-defined as liking or fondness, and `好きです` (suki desu) is used
  idiomatically for "I love you" in a romantic context.
- `愛してる` (aishiteru) is the continuous form of `愛する` (aisuru, "to love"). A Japanese
  language institute's own guidance says it is real but should not be overused; everyday
  affection in Japan is often shown through actions rather than a spoken declaration.
- `です` (desu) is polite addressee language (teineigo). Politeness tracks the addressee and the
  setting. In-group/out-group (内/外, uchi/soto) thinking shapes much of Japanese honorific use;
  for a private confession the relevant distance is closeness and setting rather than workplace
  hierarchy.

These are the claims the pack relies on. They are labelled with a support status in the
manifest, and no native-speaker review is recorded for this demo pack.

## Choosing in three steps

1. **Who is listening?** A friend, someone you respect, a partner, a spouse.
2. **How close are you?** A first confession is not the same moment as a settled partnership.
3. **Where are you?** Private and deliberate supports a heavier line; casual and public usually
   supports a lighter one.

The relationship map (`relationship-map.png`) shows the three paths used in this pack. Its full
description is in the manifest's `text_equivalent` field.

## What this lesson does not do

It does not rank the lines as "better" or "worse" Japanese, and it does not claim that every
speaker in Japan uses them the same way. Regional, generational and personal variation is real.
